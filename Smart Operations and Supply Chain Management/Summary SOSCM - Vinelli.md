## 1.1 OPERATIONS AND PROCESS MANAGEMENT
Operations management is about how organisations produce goods and services. Every product you use and every service you experience comes to you courtesy of the operations managers who organised its production. They may not always be called operations managers, but that is what they really are. They are the people who design, run and improve the processes that produce services and products for their customers.

- *OPERATIONS AND PROCESS MANAGEMENT* = activity of managing the resources and processes that produce products and services, for internal and external customers.
- *OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT* = examines how the operations function produces products and services for external customers. 
- *PROCESS* = arrangement of resources and activities that transform inputs into outputs that satisfy internal or external customer needs.
- *OPERATIONS* = transform inputs into outputs. All operations are composed of processes.

***ALL ENTERPRISES HAVE OPERATIONS***
The operations function is the part of the organization that produces products or services. All organizations have operations because all organizations produce some mix of products and services. It’s a central and important activity for any organization. An effective operations function has the potential to survive in a turbulent environment and the ability to maintain a steady improvement in its performance.

*But not all operations are the same.* 

All have operations function that produce the thing that their customers are willing to pay for. But there are often bigger differences within economic sectors than between them. The main difference between how their operations activities need to be managed is more closely related to the market position that they occupy. The implication of this is important. It means that the surface appearance of a business and its economic sector are less important to the way its operations should be managed than the intrinsic characteristics of what it is trying to achieve, such as the volume of its output, the variety of the products and services it needs to produce and, above all, how it is trying to compete in its market. Ex: hotels produce accommodation services, financial services invest and sell money.

## 1.2 PROCESS PERSPECTIVE
PROCESS PERSPECTIVE means understanding that all parts of the business can be seen as processes, and that all processes can be managed using operations management principles. The process perspective analyzes businesses as a collection of interrelated processes. Some of these processes will be within the operations function and will contribute directly to the production of its products and services; other processes will be in the other functions of the business, but will still need managing using similar principles to those within the operations function 

*ORGANIZATION* = collection of processes, interconnecting and hopefully all contributing to fulfilling its strategic aims

*OPM* $\rightarrow$ all functions have operations with their own collection of processes:
- plans
- policies
- reports
- servicies

***OPM RELEVANT TO ALL PARTS OF BUSINESS***
All part of the business manages processes, so all parts of the business have an operations role and need to understand operations management. 
Each function will have its technical knowledge, but also will have an operations role that entails using its processes to *produce plans, policies, reports and services*. So, all functions are operations with their own collection of processes.
So every manager, in all parts of an organization, is to some extent an operations manager. They all should want to give good service to their internal customers and want to do this efficiently.

***The Input***
![[file-20260930194217059.png]]

- *Transformed resource inputs:* are the resources that are changed in some way within a process. They are usually materials, information or customers. For example, one process in a bank prints statements of accounts for its customers. In doing so, it is processing materials. In the bank’s branches, customers are processed by giving them advice regarding their financial affairs, making payments, etc.
- *Transforming resource inputs:* As customers, we may be unhappy with a clunky website or if we are not treated appropriately in the bank branch. But if the bank makes errors in our financial transactions, we suffer in a far more fundamental way. There are two types of transforming resource that form the ‘building blocks’ of all processes. They are  
	- Facilities: the buildings, equipment, plant and process technology of the operation, 
	- People: who operate, maintain, plan and manage the operation.

*Transformation* 
- FRONT OFFICE: The front office parts of an operations are those processes that interact with transform customers. Customer have high visibility of the operation’s processes. 
- BACK OFFICE: The back-office operations are the processes that have little or no direct contact with customers, but perform the activities that support the front office in some way. Low customer visibility because customers receive their outputs without seeing inside the operations.

***The output***
All processes and operations produce products and/or services, and although products and services are different, the distinction can be subtlewhether classed as a product or service, the outputs from processes and operations are (or should be) intended to serve the requirements of customers. 

![[Pasted image 20260930195328.png]]

External customers provide an organization directly with its reason for being. They are the ones who provide the revenue. Serving internal customers is, in the longer term, just as important. Poor service to internal customers will eventually result in poor service to external customers, or excessive costs to the organization, or both.

***The process perspective at the level of the operation***
The operations part of a business is itself an input–transformation–output system, which transforms various inputs to produce (usually) a range of different products and services.
